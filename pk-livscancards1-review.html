Where it fits in real life

Excellent fit

Price comparison

Scan a product or shelf tag, match it to a catalogue, show several sellers side by side in the user's currency, set a drop alert.

Example: a grocery price checker that scans a pack and shows three shops' prices per kg, with an alert when one falls.
Swap: catalogue source, price feeds. Keep: index, comparison cards, alerts.
Excellent fit

Market data & collectibles

Any item with variants and a resale market: coins, stamps, sneakers, comics, vinyl, model kits.

Example: a stamp album tool that splits a page photo into stamps, IDs each by picture, and values the album with 30-day averages.
Swap: aspect ratio, ID regex. Keep: picture check, portfolio, trend chart.
Strong fit

Barcode & QR

Replace the OCR stage with the browser BarcodeDetector API (or a JS decoder); everything after the ID is reused. IDs become exact, so the pick grid is rarely needed.

Example: an ISBN shelf scanner for a school library that flags missing or duplicate books and exports a CSV stock-take.
Swap: stage 05 only.
Strong fit

Educational tools

Scan flashcards, worksheets or specimens; ID them against a curriculum list; give feedback, scores and progress bars (the "set progress" view).

Example: a vocabulary flashcard scanner that logs which cards a student owns and schedules spaced-repetition quizzes.
Swap: catalogue = syllabus; prices → scores. Keep: stack scanning, review list.
Good fit

Text mining from photos

Receipts, invoices, labels, forms. The multi-pass OCR, noisy-ID parsing and "check against a list" pattern carry over; layout extraction must be added.

Example: a receipt scanner that pulls totals and dates into an expense CSV, with the pick grid for unclear amounts.
Add: field extraction rules. Keep: OCR budgets, review flags.
Good fit

Shapes & patterns

Locate-and-measure plus colour/shape hashing works for simple shapes and repeating patterns; fine-grained species or defects need learned features.

Example: a tile or fabric pattern matcher that finds the closest catalogue design, or a coin sorter that uses diameter and colour.
Swap: locate (circles, free shapes). Upgrade: picture check → embeddings.
Good fit

Condition & quality checks

The grader's centering, edge and surface measures map to print alignment, packaging damage and label placement.

Example: a print-shop QC tool that measures margin centering on proofs and flags scuffed corners before shipping.
Retune: grader thresholds. Keep: overlay meters.
Limited fit

Handwriting

Browser OCR reads printed text, not handwriting. Use the artifact path (Claude vision) for handwriting, and keep the review and pick-grid pattern for low-confidence reads.

Example: a handwritten answer-sheet collector that groups scans by student for a teacher to review; the reading itself is done by a vision model.
Swap: stage 05 → vision model. Expect slower, metered reads.

Time and tokens saved

The basis is this project's own history: one initial build, then about 10 fix rounds over two days. About 7 of those rounds fixed problems the skill now prevents up front: blocked pickers, `[hidden]` and viewport on Pages, multi-card splitting, look-alike variants, back navigation, change-card, and catalogue download retries. One fix round in a long session is estimated at roughly 100–200k tokens and 40–60 minutes, including re-reading context and running tests.

Savings come from not repeating known failures, which are the slowest part because each needs a phone test by you. The biggest saving goes to builds closest to "photo → ID → price → list".

Limits to plan for

  • Browser OCR on stylised print and foil is the weak link; the catalogue rank plus the picture check carries most of the accuracy.
  • The picture check uses simple features. For large look-alike catalogues, precompute fingerprints (a planned Phase 2) or use embeddings.
  • Artifact mode has no network, camera or install, so live data and scanning belong in the web build.
  • Prices from public feeds lag; graded values are model estimates and must stay labelled.
  • Medical, legal or identity documents need extra safety and privacy review before reuse.

How to use it

  • Say "use pk-livscancards1" or describe a scan-and-price tool; the skill loads automatically.
  • In the PKmon repo, it reads handoff.md and extends; elsewhere, it starts from the blueprint (pk-livscancards1-blueprint.md).
  • Keep the workflow: plan, risks, approval, live %, test on real photos, PR, handoff.
